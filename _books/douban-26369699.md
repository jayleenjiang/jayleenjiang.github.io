I agree with some of its ideas, such as the claim that a superiority complex can grow from feelings of inferiority. What the authors miss is that identifying the source of an illness does not necessarily cure it; an impractical treatment can even make things worse. The idea of "chosen unhappiness" made me deeply uncomfortable. I used to be brave and forceful, and I experienced psychological trauma partly because of that. I cannot accept having that trauma described as purposeful avoidance or weakness performed to solicit sympathy. I do not lack courage. I simply refuse to let myself rest, forever forcing my naturally quiet self to swim upstream. Real courage should be rational, not a headlong charge. If every retreat is labeled fear of consequences or lack of courage, I would rather reject this reckless version of "knowing there is a tiger on the mountain and climbing it anyway." It no longer belongs within the meaning of courage.
